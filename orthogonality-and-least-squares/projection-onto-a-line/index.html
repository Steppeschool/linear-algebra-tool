Strang §4.2 · Projections

Projection onto a line: the closest point.

Given a vector b and a line through a, which point p on the line is closest to b? Drop a perpendicular from b to the line. The error e = b − p is perpendicular to a, and that one fact gives the formula.

Lesson 5 · slide 2Projection
p = x̂ a    x̂ = aᵀb / aᵀa    e = b − p ⟂ a
Key ideas
  1. The projection p lies on the line, so p = x̂a for some number x̂.
  2. The error e = b − p is perpendicular to a: aᵀ(b − x̂a) = 0.
  3. Solving for the number: x̂ = aᵀb / aᵀa.
  4. If b is on the line, p = b. If b is perpendicular to a, p = 0.

The shortest way to the road

You are standing in a field next to a long straight road and want to reach it with as few steps as possible. You do not walk at a slant. You walk straight at it, at a right angle. The spot where you arrive is the point of the road closest to you.

That is a projection. The road is the line through a, you are b, and the arrival point is p. The path you walked, e = b − p, makes a right angle with the road.

From the right angle to a formula

Two facts are enough:

  • p is on the line, so it is a multiple of a: p = x̂a. We only need the number x̂ (read "x-hat"; the hat marks the best choice).
  • e = b − x̂a is perpendicular to a, so its dot product with a is zero.
aᵀ(b − x̂a) = 0
aᵀb − x̂ aᵀa = 0
x̂ = aᵀb / aᵀa,    p = x̂a

Two dot products and one division. Nothing else is needed, in two dimensions or in a thousand.

A worked example

a = (2, 1),   b = (1, 3)
aᵀb = 2 + 3 = 5,   aᵀa = 4 + 1 = 5,   x̂ = 1
p = (2, 1),   e = b − p = (−1, 2)
check: a · e = −2 + 2 = 0 ✓

Why p is the closest point

Take any other point q on the line. The path from b to q is the path from b to p (that is −e) plus a step along the line from p to q. Those two pieces are perpendicular, so by Pythagoras the squared length is ‖e‖² plus something positive. Every other point is farther away than p.
