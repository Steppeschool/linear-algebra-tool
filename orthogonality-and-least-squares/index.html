Orthogonal Vectors and Subspaces Explained Visually

Perpendicular vectors have dot product zero. The row space is perpendicular to the null space. Examples and live Python. Strang §4.1.

Projection Onto a Line: Formula and Picture

Project b onto the line through a: p = (aᵀb / aᵀa) a, the closest point, with the error perpendicular to a. Examples and Python. Strang §4.2.

Lesson 5Projection matrixProjection matrices: P flattens everything onto the line.Open slide →03 · Strang §4.2 · Projection matrices

Projection Matrix P = A(AᵀA)⁻¹Aᵀ Explained

The projection onto a line is Pb with P = aaᵀ/aᵀa. Onto the column space of A, P = A(AᵀA)⁻¹Aᵀ. Why P² = P, with examples and Python. Strang §4.2.

Lesson 5Least squaresLeast squares: the best answer when there is no exact one.Open slide →04 · Strang §4.3 · Least squares approximations

Least Squares and the Normal Equations AᵀAx = Aᵀb

When Ax = b has no solution, least squares finds the best x̂ by solving AᵀAx̂ = Aᵀb. Why it works, worked examples and Python. Strang §4.3.

Lesson 5Line fittingFitting a straight line to sensor data.Open slide →05 · Strang §4.3 · Fitting a straight line

Fitting a Line to Data with Least Squares (Interactive)

Fit the best straight line C + Dt to noisy sensor data. Drag C and D to shrink the squared error, then solve the normal equations. Strang §4.3.

Lesson 5Orthonormal basesOrthonormal vectors and Q: the easiest basis to work with.Open slide →06 · Strang §4.4 · Orthonormal bases

Orthonormal Basis and Orthogonal Matrices (QᵀQ = I)

Orthonormal vectors are perpendicular with length 1. In Q they give QᵀQ = I and Q⁻¹ = Qᵀ. Rotations, examples and Python. Strang §4.4.

Lesson 5Gram-SchmidtGram-Schmidt: making any basis orthonormal.Open slide →07 · Strang §4.4 · The Gram-Schmidt process

Gram-Schmidt Process Step by Step (and A = QR)

Gram-Schmidt turns independent vectors into an orthonormal basis: subtract projections, divide by length, and get A = QR. Strang §4.4.
