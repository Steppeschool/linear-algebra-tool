Strang §4.3 · Fitting a straight line

Fitting a straight line to sensor data.

Measurements are noisy, so the points never sit exactly on a line. The best line C + Dt makes the sum of squared vertical errors as small as possible. The normal equations give C and D in one step, for four points or four million.

Lesson 5 · slide 5Line fitting
E = Σ (bᵢ − C − D tᵢ)²    mΣtΣtΣt² (C, D) = (Σb, Σtb)
Key ideas
  1. Each data point (tᵢ, bᵢ) gives one equation C + D tᵢ = bᵢ.
  2. A has a column of ones (for C) and a column of times tᵢ (for D).
  3. The errors are the vertical gaps eᵢ = bᵢ − (C + D tᵢ). Least squares minimizes the sum of their squares.
  4. The normal equations become two equations in C and D, built from m and four sums.
  5. The same method fits calibration lines, trends and growth rates.

A ruler across the pins

Picture a corkboard with a few pins and a ruler laid across them. You slide and tilt the ruler until it looks as close as possible to all the pins at once. Least squares makes "as close as possible" precise. Measure each vertical gap, square it, add the squares, and make that total E as small as possible.

The lab does exactly this. Drag C (the value of the line at t = 0) and D (its slope) and watch E. The dashed grey line is the best one. When you land on it, the readout says so.

The data

A motor is switched on and its temperature rise is read once a minute: t = 0, 1, 2, 3 minutes, readings b = 1, 2, 4, 5 degrees. The points are close to a line but not on one.

A = 10111213,   b = (1, 2, 4, 5)

The normal equations

Where do the sums come from? The two rows of Aᵀ are the column of ones and the column of times. So every entry of AᵀA and Aᵀb is a dot product of two columns:

ones · ones = m, the number of points    ones · t = Σt, the sum of the times    t · t = Σt², the sum of the squared times
ones · b = Σb, the sum of the readings    t · b = Σtᵢbᵢ, each time multiplied by its reading, then added
AᵀA = mΣtΣtΣt²,   Aᵀb = (Σb, Σtᵢbᵢ)

For the motor:

m = 4,   Σt = 6,   Σt² = 14,   Σb = 12,   Σtᵢbᵢ = 0 + 2 + 8 + 15 = 25
4C + 6D = 12    6C + 14D = 25
Multiply the first by 1.5 and subtract: 5D = 7, so D = 1.4 and C = 0.9.   Best line: 0.9 + 1.4t
predictions (0.9, 2.3, 3.7, 5.1),   errors (0.1, −0.3, 0.3, −0.1),   E = 0.2

The motor warms by about 1.4 degrees per minute. The errors add up to zero, as the last slide predicted.
