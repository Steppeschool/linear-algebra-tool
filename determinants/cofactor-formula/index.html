Strang §5.2 · Permutations and cofactors

Cofactor expansion: a big determinant from smaller ones

Slide 5 found det A fast by elimination, but the answer came out of a process, not a formula. Cofactors give the formula. Think of a big job shared among helpers: each entry in row 1 hands its share of the work to a smaller determinant. To find that share, cross out the entry's row and its column. The smaller determinant that is left is the entry's minor. Give it a sign from the pattern + − +, and you have the entry's cofactor. Multiply each entry by its cofactor and add: that is det A. By hand this is great for 3 by 3, and hopeless for big matrices.

Lesson 6 · slide 6Cofactors
det A = a₁₁C₁₁ + a₁₂C₁₂ + a₁₃C₁₃    C₁ⱼ = (−1)^(1+j) · det(A without row 1 and column j)
Key ideas
  1. The minor of entry aᵢⱼ is the determinant of A with row i and column j crossed out.
  2. The cofactor Cᵢⱼ is that minor times (−1)^(i+j): signs + − + in row 1, − + − in row 2, and so on, like a checkerboard.
  3. Expanding along any row (or column): det A = sum of entries × their cofactors.
  4. Fully expanded, a 3 by 3 determinant is a sum of 3! = 6 products. Each uses one entry from every row and every column. An n by n has n! of them.

Where the number comes from

Elimination gives det A fast. The cofactor formula shows what det A is made of: a sum of products, each using one entry from every row and every column.

The 2 by 2 case

For abcd there are two ways to pick one entry from each row, all in different columns: a with d (columns 1, 2) or b with c (columns 2, 1). The second order is one swap away, so rule 2 gives it a minus: det = ad − bc.

3124:   3·4 − 1·2 = 12 − 2 = 10

Expanding a 3 by 3 along row 1

aᵢⱼ is the entry in row i, column j. Take each entry of row 1 and cross out its row and column. The 2 by 2 left over is its minor. Multiply entry × minor, with signs + − +:

det A = a₁₁(a₂₂a₃₃ − a₂₃a₃₂) − a₁₂(a₂₁a₃₃ − a₂₃a₃₁) + a₁₃(a₂₁a₃₂ − a₂₂a₃₁)

Each minor with its sign is a cofactor. Try it on the matrix K from lesson 2:

K = 2114−60−272
2·((−6)·2 − 0·7) − 1·(4·2 − 0·(−2)) + 1·(4·7 − (−6)·(−2))
= 2·(−12) − 1·8 + 1·16 = −24 − 8 + 16 = −16 ✓ (the same as the pivots 2 · (−8) · 1)

The checkerboard of signs

The signs form a checkerboard, with + in the top left. The sign for row i, column j is (−1)^(i+j):

+−+−+−+−+   row 2, column 1: (−1)^(2+1) = −1

You may expand along any row or any column, as long as you use these signs. Along row 2 of K, the signs are − + −:

−4·(1·2 − 1·7) + (−6)·(2·2 − 1·(−2)) − 0·(…)
= −4·(−5) − 6·6 − 0 = 20 − 36 = −16 ✓

Pick the easiest row

A zero entry kills its whole term, so pick the row or column with the most zeros. In K, row 2 has a 0, so only two minors were needed above.
