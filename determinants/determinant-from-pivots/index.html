Strang §5.1 · Determinants by elimination

Eliminate, then multiply the pivots.

Elimination does not change the determinant, and a triangular matrix has an easy determinant: the product of its diagonal. So det A is the product of the pivots, with a minus sign for each row exchange. This is how computers find determinants.

Lesson 6 · slide 3Det from pivots
det A = ± (pivot₁ × pivot₂ × ... × pivotₙ)    det AB = det A · det B    det Aᵀ = det A
Key ideas
  1. Elimination steps (subtract a multiple of a row) keep det the same.
  2. Each row exchange flips the sign.
  3. For a triangular matrix, multiply down the diagonal.
  4. So det A = ± the product of the pivots. A zero pivot that no swap can fix means det A = 0.
  5. Two more facts: det AB = det A · det B, and det Aᵀ = det A.
