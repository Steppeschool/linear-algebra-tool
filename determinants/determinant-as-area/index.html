Strang §5.1 · What the determinant measures

The determinant: how much A stretches area.

Every square matrix has one number attached to it, the determinant. It answers a geometric question: by what factor does the matrix stretch areas (in 2D) or volumes (in 3D)? A negative sign means space is also flipped, and zero means it is squashed flat.

Lesson 6 · slide 1Determinant
det A = ad − bc for A = abcd    area of image = |det A| × area
Key ideas
  1. det A is the factor by which A multiplies every area (2D) or volume (3D).
  2. det A < 0: the matrix also flips space, like a mirror.
  3. det A = 0: space is squashed into a line or a plane. Then A has no inverse.
  4. For 2 by 2, det A = ad − bc. Larger matrices follow from three rules (slide 2).
