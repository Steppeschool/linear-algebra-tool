An independent set that reaches the whole space is a basis, and every basis of a space has the same number of vectors: the dimension. independent + spans the space = basis    size of every basis = dimension
  1. Independent: the only combination giving 0 is the one with all coefficients 0.
  2. Span: all combinations of the vectors.
  3. Basis: independent vectors that span the space.
  4. Dimension: the number of vectors in any basis. The rank r is the dimension of C(A).

Think of a minimal toolkit

A good toolkit lets you do every job, and contains no tool that another tool already covers. "Every job can be done" is the vectors spanning the space: every vector in it is some combination of them. "No redundant tool" is the vectors being independent. Both together make a basis.

The dimension is how many tools that takes. A point on a map needs exactly two numbers, and a drone in the air needs three. That count does not depend on which directions we choose.

Testing for independence

Put the vectors in the columns of A. These statements all mean the same thing:

  • The only solution of Ax = 0 is x = 0.
  • N(A) contains only the zero vector.
  • Every column has a pivot, so the rank is r = n.

We write ℝⁿ for all vectors with n components: ℝ² is the plane and ℝ³ is 3D space.

Four vectors in ℝ³ are always dependent. Put them in the columns of a 3 by 4 matrix. Each pivot needs its own row, so there are at most 3 pivots for 4 columns. At least one column is free, and its special solution is a nonzero x with Ax = 0.

A worked example

v₁ = (1, 0, 1), v₂ = (0, 1, 1), v₃ = (1, 1, 2).
v₃ = v₁ + v₂,  so 1·v₁ + 1·v₂ − 1·v₃ = 0
The vectors are dependent and the rank is 2. All three lie in one plane, and v₁, v₂ alone form a basis for that plane. Its dimension is 2.

A basis is not unique

(1, 0) and (0, 1) form a basis of the plane, and so do (1, 1) and (1, −1). The vectors differ, but there are always two of them. In general, the pivot columns of A form a basis of its column space, and their number is the rank.
