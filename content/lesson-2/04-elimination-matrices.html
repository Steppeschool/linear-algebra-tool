Subtracting ℓ times row 1 from row 2 is the same as multiplying by E₂₁: the identity matrix with −ℓ in row 2, column 1. The identity matrix I has 1s on the diagonal and 0s elsewhere, so IA = A: it changes nothing. E₂₁ = 100−ℓ10001    E₂₁A = A with row 2 − ℓ·(row 1)
  1. An elimination matrix E is the identity with one extra entry, −ℓ, below the diagonal.
  2. Multiplying EA performs that row operation on A.
  3. A row of EA is a combination of the rows of A.
  4. To undo E, change −ℓ to +ℓ. This is the key to A = LU on the next slide.

Why write a step as a matrix?

So far elimination has been a list of instructions: "subtract 2 times row 1 from row 2", "add row 1 to row 3", and so on. Matrices let us write the whole process as one equation, and then reason about it with the rules from lesson 1.

Think of an instruction card

A row operation is like a card with an instruction on it: "take row 2, subtract 2 times row 1, leave the other rows alone". The matrix E₂₁ is that card written in numbers, and multiplying by it means following the card.

E₂₁ = 100−210001

Why it works: rows combine rows

In lesson 1 we read Ax by columns. For EA it is easier to read by rows: row i of EA is row i of E times A, which is a combination of the rows of A.

  • Row 1 of E₂₁ is (1, 0, 0): it keeps row 1 of A unchanged.
  • Row 2 is (−2, 1, 0): it produces row 2 minus 2 times row 1.
  • Row 3 is (0, 0, 1): it keeps row 3 unchanged.

Here is row 2 written out once, with A from slide 2. The row (−2, 1, 0) times A takes −2 of row 1, 1 of row 2 and 0 of row 3:

(−2, 1, 0) A = −2·(2, 1, 1) + 1·(4, −6, 0) + 0·(−2, 7, 2) = (0, −8, −2)

That is exactly the new row 2 from slide 2.

The whole process as one product

Each step is one card, and elimination applies them one after another:

E₃₂ E₃₁ E₂₁ A = U

Read it from right to left, as in lesson 1: E₂₁ acts first.

Undoing a step

To undo "subtract 2 times row 1 from row 2", we add 2 times row 1 back. The matrix that undoes E₂₁ is written E₂₁⁻¹ ("E₂₁ inverse"); lesson 3 is about such inverses. Here E₂₁⁻¹ is the identity with +2 in the same position. Every elimination matrix is undone by flipping the sign of its one extra entry. The next slide uses this to turn elimination into a factorization of A.

Example 1 · A 2 by 2 step

A = 3164. The multiplier is ℓ = 6 ÷ 3 = 2.

E₂₁ = 10−21    E₂₁A = 3102

Example 2 · The third step, E₃₂

After the first two steps, B = 2110−8−2083. Clear the 8 with ℓ₃₂ = 8 ÷ (−8) = −1.

E₃₂ = 100010011   (−ℓ₃₂ = +1 in row 3, column 2)
E₃₂B = 2110−8−2001 = U

Example 3 · Undoing a step

E₂₁ = 100−210001 subtracts 2·row 1 from row 2.

E₂₁⁻¹ = 100210001 adds it back
E₂₁⁻¹E₂₁ = I

Only the sign of the multiplier changes.
