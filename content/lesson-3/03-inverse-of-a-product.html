To undo it, you undo the last move first: A⁻¹ first, then B⁻¹. Written as a product, that is B⁻¹A⁻¹. (AB)⁻¹ = B⁻¹A⁻¹     (ABC)⁻¹ = C⁻¹B⁻¹A⁻¹

Think: socks and shoes, taken off

You put on socks, then shoes. In the evening you take off the shoes first, then the socks. Same two steps, reverse order.

Matrix products read right to left. AB puts on B first. So the inverse takes off A first, and that is written B⁻¹A⁻¹.

Check it with algebra

(B⁻¹A⁻¹)(AB) = B⁻¹(A⁻¹A)B = B⁻¹ I B = B⁻¹B = I ✓
The A and its inverse meet in the middle and cancel. Then B and its inverse cancel.

Worked example

R turns by 90°, S is a shear. From lesson 1, RS = 0−111
R⁻¹ = 01−10 (turn back)   S⁻¹ = 1−101 (slide back)
S⁻¹R⁻¹ = 11−10   but   R⁻¹S⁻¹ = 01−11
Only the first one undoes RS.

Where you meet this

A robot arm's gripper position is a product of joint rotations, base first. To convert a point from the gripper's view back to the base, you undo the joints from the gripper inwards: last joint first.

A rotation is undone by turning back, and that matrix is also its transpose. Slide 5 explains why that matters.

import numpy as np R = np.array([[0., -1.], [1., 0.]]) # turn 90° S = np.array([[1., 1.], [0., 1.]]) # shear R_inv = np.linalg.inv(R) S_inv = np.linalg.inv(S) undo = R_inv @ S_inv # is this the right order? print("undo @ (R @ S) =", undo @ (R @ S)) show(undo)
