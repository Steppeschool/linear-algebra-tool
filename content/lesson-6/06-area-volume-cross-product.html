So the area of any triangle comes from the determinant of two edge vectors, the volume of a box from three, and the cross product of two 3D vectors is built from 2 by 2 determinants. triangle area = ½ |det [edge₁ edge₂]|    box volume = |det [u v w]|    ‖u × v‖ = parallelogram area
  1. Area of the parallelogram spanned by u and v: |det [u v]|. The triangle is half of it.
  2. Use edge vectors (corner minus corner), not the corners themselves.
  3. Volume of the box with edges u, v, w: |det [u v w]|.

Measuring a field from GPS corners

A farmer walks to the three corners of a triangular field with a GPS. How big is the field? No tape measure needed: the area is a determinant.

From corners to area

Put the first corner at the origin by subtracting it from the others. The two results are edge vectors. They span a parallelogram of area |det|, and the triangle is exactly half of it.

Corners (1, 1), (4, 2), (2, 5)
Edges: (4, 2) − (1, 1) = (3, 1),   (2, 5) − (1, 1) = (1, 4)
det 3114 = 12 − 1 = 11,   area = 11/2 = 5.5

Using the corners directly would measure a triangle that has a corner at the origin, a different shape. That is the mistake the lab asks you to fix.

Volume of a box

Three edge vectors u, v, w from one corner span a slanted box. Its volume is |det| of the 3 by 3 matrix with u, v, w as columns (slide 1). A volume of 0 means the three edges lie in one plane.

The cross product

For two vectors in 3D there is a third vector, u × v, that is perpendicular to both. Its three components are 2 by 2 determinants built from u and v:

u × v = (u₂v₃ − u₃v₂,   u₃v₁ − u₁v₃,   u₁v₂ − u₂v₁)
(1, 2, 3) × (0, 1, 4) = (8 − 3, 0 − 4, 1 − 0) = (5, −4, 1)
Check: (5, −4, 1) · (1, 2, 3) = 5 − 8 + 3 = 0 ✓   (5, −4, 1) · (0, 1, 4) = 0 − 4 + 4 = 0 ✓

Its length equals the area of the parallelogram spanned by u and v, and its direction follows the right-hand rule: curl the fingers from u to v, and the thumb points along u × v. Swapping u and v flips it: v × u = −(u × v).

Where this is used

Torque is a cross product. Push with force F at position r from a joint, and the twist on the joint is τ = r × F. A robot arm controller computes exactly this to know how hard each motor must work. Graphics and 3D printers use u × v to find the outward normal of every triangle in a mesh.

Example 1 · Torque on a joint

A force F = (1, 3, 0) N acts at r = (2, 0, 0) m from a joint.

τ = r × F = (0·0 − 0·3,   0·1 − 2·0,   2·3 − 0·1) = (0, 0, 6) N·m
The twist is about the z-axis, of size 6 N·m

Only the sideways part of the force (3 N, at a 2 m lever) twists the joint: 2 × 3 = 6.

Example 2 · A box volume

Edges u = (1, 0, 0), v = (1, 2, 0), w = (0, 1, 3).

det 110021003 (edges as columns) = 1 · 2 · 3 = 6
Triangular, so the product of the diagonal. Volume 6

Example 3 · Points on one line

Corners (0, 0), (1, 2), (2, 4).

Edges (1, 2) and (2, 4): det = 4 − 4 = 0
Area 0: the three points lie on one line
