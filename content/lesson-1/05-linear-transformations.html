  1. A matrix sends each vector v to a new vector Av. Applied to every point, it moves the whole plane.
  2. Lines stay straight, evenly spaced lines stay evenly spaced, and the origin stays fixed.
  3. Column 1 is where (1, 0) lands. Column 2 is where (0, 1) lands. Those two facts decide everything.
  4. The area of any shape is multiplied by |det A|.

Think of graph paper

Pin graph paper at the origin. You may stretch, turn, slant or flip it. You may not bend, fold or tear it. Every 2 by 2 matrix does some mix of these. A singular matrix (determinant 0, the ps − qr from slide 3) can also squash the paper flat onto a line, or onto the origin.

Why no bending? Because A(cv + dw) = cAv + dAw. Each entry of Ax is a row times the vector (slide 2), and row·(cv + dw) = c(row·v) + d(row·w). So evenly spaced points on a line stay evenly spaced on a line:

points v + t·w (t = 0, 1, 2, …)  →  Av + t·Aw: again a line, with the same even step Aw

Two landmarks decide everything

Every point is (x, y) = x·(1, 0) + y·(0, 1). Since A respects combinations,

A(x, y) = x·A(1, 0) + y·A(0, 1) = x·(column 1) + y·(column 2)

Know where (1, 0) and (0, 1) go, and you know where every point goes. To build a matrix, choose where they land and write those as the columns.

Building a rotation

To turn the plane counterclockwise by θ, move both landmarks around the unit circle. (1, 0) is at 0°, so it lands at θ: (cos θ, sin θ). (0, 1) is at 90°, so it lands at 90° + θ: (cos(90° + θ), sin(90° + θ)) = (−sin θ, cos θ). Those are the columns:

Q = cos θ−sin θsin θcos θ
For a quarter turn, θ = 90°: cos θ = 0 and sin θ = 1, so Q = 0−110. It sends (1, 0) to (0, 1) and (0, 1) to (−1, 0).
Check with a picture: turn the arrow (1, 0) a quarter turn counterclockwise and it points straight up, at (0, 1). Turn (0, 1) the same way and it points left, at (−1, 0). The formula agrees.

What happens to areas

Watch the shaded unit square in the lab. It becomes a parallelogram with area |det A| = |ps − qr|. Every shape's area is multiplied by that number (Strang §5.3).

A negative determinant means the plane was flipped face down. Slide 7 builds such a flip. A rotation has det Q = cos²θ + sin²θ = 1: it turns without stretching.

Where this is used

A drone's IMU (inertial measurement unit) is a sensor chip that measures motion along the drone's own forward, sideways and up axes. A 3 by 3 rotation matrix turns those readings into north, east and up. The flight software keeps that matrix up to date as the drone tilts and turns.

Example 1 · Stretch sideways

Triple every x-coordinate, keep y: (1, 0) → (3, 0) and (0, 1) → (0, 1).

A = 3001    det A = 3
(1, 1) → (3, 1). Every area becomes 3 times larger

Example 2 · Half a turn

Rotate by 180°: cos 180° = −1, sin 180° = 0.

Q = −100−1    det Q = 1
(2, 1) → (−2, −1): every vector points the opposite way

Example 3 · Squash onto a line

Where does A = 1212 send the plane?

(1, 0) → (1, 1) and (0, 1) → (2, 2): both columns lie on the line y = x
det A = 1·2 − 2·1 = 0

Every point lands on the line y = x, and every area becomes 0. This is the dependent case from slide 3, seen as a motion.
