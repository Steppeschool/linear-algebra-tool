To multiply it by a vector x, there are two ways, and they always give the same answer. By rows: dot each row of A with x (slide 2), and each dot product gives one number of the answer. By columns: take x₁ of column 1, plus x₂ of column 2, a linear combination like on slide 1. Rows are the quick way to compute. Columns are the way to understand what Ax can be. a₁₁a₁₂a₂₁a₂₂ x₁x₂ = x₁ a₁₁a₂₁ + x₂ a₁₂a₂₂
  1. Row way: entry i of Ax is row i of A dotted with x.
  2. Column way: Ax = x₁·(column 1) + x₂·(column 2), a combination of the columns.
  3. Both ways add up the same products, so they always agree.
  4. x needs one number per column of A. Ax has one number per row of A.

One example, two ways

Take

A = 2−111    x = (3, 1)

Way 1, by rows. Each row of A is dotted with x. Row 1 gives the top number of the answer, row 2 the bottom number:

row 1: 2−1 · (3, 1) = 2·3 + (−1)·1 = 6 − 1 = 5
row 2: 11 · (3, 1) = 1·3 + 1·1 = 3 + 1 = 4
Ax = (5, 4)

Way 2, by columns. x = (3, 1) says: take 3 of column 1 and 1 of column 2, and add:

Ax = 3·(2, 1) + 1·(−1, 1) = (6, 3) + (−1, 1) = (5, 4)

Same answer, (5, 4). It has to be: both ways add the same four products, just in a different order. On the plane, the column way is a walk: 3 steps of (2, 1), then 1 step of (−1, 1).

A note on writing: aij is the entry in row i, column j, so a₁₂ is in row 1, column 2. And (3, 1) is written across to save space, but a vector is always a column: 3 on top, 1 below.

An everyday picture: a shopping basket

An egg has about 6 g of protein and 1 g of carbohydrate. A bowl of rice has about 4 g and 45 g. Each food is a column:

A = 64145    x = (2 eggs, 1 bowl)

The basket holds 2·(6, 1) + 1·(4, 45) = (16, 47): 16 g of protein, 47 g of carbohydrate. That total is Ax. Adding whole foods, one column at a time, is the column way.

The basket by rows

Or add one nutrient at a time. Protein: 6·2 + 4·1 = 16. Carbohydrate: 1·2 + 45·1 = 47. Each is a dot product of a row of A with x.

Both ways add the same products, so they always agree. Rows are quicker by hand. Columns show what Ax can be.

Watch the sizes

A vector with n entries is an n by 1 matrix. x needs one entry per column of A, and Ax has one entry per row. So a 2 by 3 matrix (2 rows, 3 columns) takes in 3 numbers and gives back 2.

(2 × 3) · (3 × 1) → (2 × 1)    the inner numbers must match, the outer numbers give the size of the answer

A bigger example: 4 nutrients, 3 foods

Add an apple, and track fat and fiber too. Now A has 4 rows (nutrients) and 3 columns (foods):

A = 64014525500014    x = (2 eggs, 1 bowl, 1 apple)
Ax = 2·(6, 1, 5, 0) + 1·(4, 45, 0, 1) + 1·(0, 25, 0, 4) = (16, 72, 10, 5)

16 g protein, 72 g carbs, 10 g fat, 5 g fiber. Same idea, bigger table: 3 numbers go in, 4 come out, because (4 × 3) · (3 × 1) → (4 × 1). A matrix can have any number of rows and columns.

Example 1 · Both ways

A = 3125, x = (1, 2).

By columns: 1·(3, 2) + 2·(1, 5) = (5, 12)
By rows: (3·1 + 1·2, 2·1 + 5·2) = (5, 12)

Example 2 · A wide matrix

A = 102311 has 3 columns, so x needs 3 entries. Take x = (2, 1, 1). (2 × 3) · (3 × 1) → (2 × 1)

Ax = 2·(1, 3) + 1·(0, 1) + 1·(2, 1) = (4, 8)

Three columns in, a vector with 2 entries out: one per row of A.

Example 3 · Picking out a column

What is A times x = (1, 0)? And times (0, 1)?

A(1, 0) = 1·(column 1) + 0·(column 2) = column 1
A(0, 1) = column 2

So the columns of A are exactly what A does to (1, 0) and (0, 1). We use this again in lesson 6, where a matrix moves the whole plane.

Example 4 · Two motors on a drone

Look at a drone from behind and keep only two motors, left and right. We track two numbers: the lift (the total upward push) and the tilt (right push minus left push; positive means the right side rises). Each motor gives one column:

M = 11−11   column 1: left motor, column 2: right motor
Both motors at 2:   M(2, 2) = 2·(1, −1) + 2·(1, 1) = (4, 0)
Left 1, right 3:   M(1, 3) = 1·(1, −1) + 3·(1, 1) = (4, 2)

The lift stays 4, so the drone holds its height, but now the right side rises. The output is a mix of the motor columns, weighted by the thrusts.
