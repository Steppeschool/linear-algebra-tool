The F keeps its size. It only faces the other way. (x, y)  →  (−x, y)

Think: the word on an ambulance

AMBULANCE is printed backwards on the front of the vehicle. In a driver's mirror it reads the right way round. A mirror keeps sizes and distances but reverses left and right.

How to build it

Use the rule from slide 5: decide where the two landmarks land, then write them as the columns.

  • A mirror on the vertical axis sends points on the right over to the left.
  • Points on the vertical axis itself stay where they are.

What the determinant says

The F keeps its size, so |det A| = 1.

But the F now reads backwards, like turning a sheet of paper over to its other side. No rotation can do that. The determinant signals the flip with a minus sign. Watch the readout under the plane.

After you solve it

  • Mirror twice: two mirrors in a barber's shop show your face the right way round. Check that A @ A gives the identity.
  • Another mirror: try the mirror on the diagonal line y = x. Where do (1, 0) and (0, 1) go?

Example 1 · Mirror on y = x

(1, 0) → (0, 1) and (0, 1) → (1, 0).

M = 0110    det M = −1
(2, 1) → (1, 2): the coordinates swap

Example 2 · Two mirrors make a turn

Mirror on the y-axis, then on the x-axis:

−1001 100−1 = −100−1
det = (−1)·(−1) = 1

Two flips cancel. The result is the half turn: no mirror image left.
