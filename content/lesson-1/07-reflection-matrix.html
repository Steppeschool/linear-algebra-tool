The F should keep its size and simply face the other way. (x, y)  →  (−x, y)
  1. To build a matrix, decide where (1, 0) and (0, 1) land and use them as columns (slide 5).
  2. A mirror keeps lengths and areas, so |det A| = 1.
  3. A mirror flips the plane over, and the determinant shows that with a minus sign: det A = −1.
  4. Applying the same mirror twice brings everything back: A·A = I. Here I = 1001 is the identity matrix, which leaves every vector where it is.

A mirror you have seen

The word AMBULANCE is printed backwards on the front of the vehicle, so that a driver sees it the right way round in the mirror. A mirror keeps every size and every distance, but swaps left and right.

How to build it

Use the rule from slide 5. Ask where the two landmarks should go:

  • A mirror standing on the vertical axis sends points on the right over to the left. So (1, 0) goes to (−1, 0).
  • Points on the vertical axis itself do not move. So (0, 1) stays at (0, 1).

Those two answers are the columns of the matrix. Write them in, run the code, and watch the F.

What the determinant tells us

The F keeps its size, so the area factor |det A| must be 1. But the F now reads backwards. That is like turning a sheet of paper over to its other side, and no rotation can do it. The determinant records the flip with a minus sign: a mirror has det A = −1. Look at the readout under the picture.

After you solve it

  • Mirror twice. In a barber's shop, two mirrors show your face the right way round. Check in code that A @ A gives the identity matrix.
  • Another mirror. Try the mirror on the diagonal line y = x. Where do (1, 0) and (0, 1) go now?

Example 1 · Mirror on y = x

(1, 0) → (0, 1) and (0, 1) → (1, 0).

M = 0110    det M = −1
(2, 1) → (1, 2): the coordinates swap

Example 2 · Two mirrors make a turn

Mirror on the x-axis first, then on the y-axis (the right-hand matrix acts first):

−1001 100−1 = −100−1
det = (−1)·(−1) = 1

Two flips cancel. The result is the half turn: no mirror image left.

Example 3 · Rotation or mirror?

Is 01−10 a rotation or a mirror?

det = 0·0 − 1·(−1) = 1
(1, 0) → (0, −1) and (0, 1) → (1, 0)

The determinant is +1, so nothing is flipped. It is a rotation: a quarter turn clockwise.
