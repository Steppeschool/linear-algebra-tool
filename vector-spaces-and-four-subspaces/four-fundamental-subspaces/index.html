Strang §3.5 · Dimensions of the four subspaces

Four subspaces, one big picture.

Every matrix has four subspaces. Two live among the inputs: the row space and the nullspace. Two live among the outputs: the column space and the left nullspace. The rank r sets the size of all four.

Lesson 4 · slide 6Four subspaces
inputs (ℝⁿ):  row space r + nullspace n − r   |   outputs (ℝᵐ):  column space r + left nullspace m − r
Key ideas
  1. Inputs (ℝⁿ, all vectors with n components): the row space C(Aᵀ) (all combinations of the rows), dimension r, and the nullspace N(A), dimension n − r.
  2. Outputs (ℝᵐ): the column space C(A), dimension r, and the left nullspace N(Aᵀ) (all y with Aᵀy = 0), dimension m − r.
  3. The row space and the column space always have the same dimension, r.
  4. The row space is perpendicular to the nullspace.

The two new subspaces

You know the column space C(A) and the nullspace N(A). Two more come from the transpose Aᵀ:

  • The row space is all combinations of the rows of A. The rows of A are the columns of Aᵀ, so we write it C(Aᵀ).
  • The left nullspace N(Aᵀ) is all y with Aᵀy = 0. That is the same as yᵀA = 0: y multiplies A from the left.

Think of a shadow

The sun is straight overhead, and each object casts a shadow on the floor. Object to shadow is like a matrix taking 3D inputs to 2D outputs.

  • Moving the object straight up or down doesn't change its shadow. That lost direction is the nullspace.
  • Moving it along the floor moves the shadow the same amount. That part of the input is the row space.
  • Every place a shadow can fall is the column space.
  • Here that is the whole floor, so no output direction is missed and the left nullspace holds only zero. It grows when the column space is smaller than the output space, as in the example below.

Why the row space is perpendicular to the nullspace

Ax = 0 says each row of A, dotted with x, gives 0. So x is perpendicular to every row, and to every combination of rows: the whole row space. The same argument on Aᵀ shows the left nullspace is perpendicular to the column space. The next lesson (Strang, chapter 4) builds on this.
