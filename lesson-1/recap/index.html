Recap · Reading

What you can now do.

Six ideas, all built on one operation: combining columns.

Ax = x₁·(column 1) + x₂·(column 2)
  • §1.1 Linear combinations. Scale and add: steps of v and steps of w.
  • §1.3 Ax by columns. The basket: each column is a food, x is how many of each, Ax is the total. Compute by rows, think by columns.
  • §1.3, §3.1 Column space. Everywhere the boat can reach. Ax = b is solvable exactly when b is in C(A).
  • §2.1 Row and column pictures. Two bearings crossing, or a recipe that makes b. Same answer.
  • §8.1, §5.3 Transformations. Graph paper pinned at the origin. The columns are where the two landmarks land, and |det A| is the area factor.
  • §2.4 Products. Socks, then shoes. AB applies B first.

Next lesson: elimination (§2.2, §2.3, §2.6)

Solve Ax = b the way a computer does. Subtract multiples of one row from another until the matrix is triangular. Then solve from the bottom up. Write those steps as matrices and you get A = LU.
