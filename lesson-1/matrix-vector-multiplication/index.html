Strang §1.3 · Matrices

Ax is a combination of the columns of A.

Stack v and w side by side as the columns of a matrix A. Put the numbers c and d into a vector x. Then Ax is exactly cv + dw.

a₁₁a₁₂a₂₁a₂₂ x₁x₂ = x₁ a₁₁a₂₁ + x₂ a₁₂a₂₂

Think: a shopping basket

You buy eggs and rice. Each food gives you roughly this much protein and carbs:

1 egg = (6 g protein, 1 g carbs)    1 bowl of rice = (4 g protein, 45 g carbs)
A = 64145   x = (2 eggs, 1 bowl)

Each column of A is one food. The vector x says how many of each you take. Ax is the total in your basket.

Two ways to add it up

  • By columns (whole foods): 2 eggs + 1 bowl = 2·(6, 1) + 1·(4, 45) = (16, 47).
  • By rows (one nutrient at a time): protein 6·2 + 4·1 = 16. Carbs 1·2 + 45·1 = 47.

Same totals. The row way is how you calculate by hand: each entry is a dot product of a row with x. The column way is how you think: Ax is a mix of the columns.

Strang's advice: compute by rows if you like, but think by columns. The column view tells you what Ax can be.

Watch out

x needs one number per column. Two foods, two quantities. A 2 by 2 matrix needs a vector with 2 entries.

In the lab, the code computes Ax both ways. The plane draws the column way: x₁ times column 1, then x₂ times column 2 from its tip.
