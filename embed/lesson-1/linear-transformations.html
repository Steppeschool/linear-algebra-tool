Strang §8.1 · Linear transformations

A matrix also moves the whole plane.

A matrix can also be seen as a machine that moves points. Feed in a vector v and out comes Av. Do this to every point at once and the whole plane moves.

Lesson 1 · slide 5Transformations
A 10 = column 1     A 01 = column 2
Key ideas
  1. A matrix sends each vector v to a new vector Av. Applied to every point, it moves the whole plane.
  2. Lines stay straight, evenly spaced lines stay evenly spaced, and the origin stays fixed.
  3. Column 1 is where 10 lands. Column 2 is where 01 lands. Those two facts decide everything.
  4. The area of any shape is multiplied by |det A|.

Think of graph paper

Pin a sheet of graph paper at the origin. You may stretch it, turn it, slant it, or flip it over. You may not bend it, fold it or tear it. Every 2 by 2 matrix does some mix of these motions. A matrix with determinant 0 (the number ps − qr from slide 3) is called singular. A singular matrix can also squash the paper flat onto a line, or onto the origin.

Why can't a matrix bend the paper? Because matrices respect combinations: A(cv + dw) = cAv + dAw. The reason is the row way from slide 2: each entry is a row times the vector, and ordinary algebra gives row·(cv + dw) = c(row·v) + d(row·w). Points that were evenly spaced along a straight line stay evenly spaced along a straight line:

points v + t·w (t = 0, 1, 2, …)  →  Av + t·Aw: again a line, with the same even step Aw

Two landmarks decide everything

Every point can be written as a combination of the two basic vectors: xy = x·10 + y·01. Since A respects combinations,

A(x, y) = x·A(1, 0) + y·A(0, 1) = x·(column 1) + y·(column 2)

This is slide 2 again, seen in a new way. If we know where the two landmarks 10 and 01 go, we know where every point goes.

That also tells us how to build a matrix: decide where 10 and 01 should land, and write those two vectors as the columns.

Building a rotation

To turn the plane by an angle θ (counterclockwise), follow the two landmarks around the unit circle. 10 sits at angle 0°, so it lands at angle θ: the point cos θsin θ. 01 sits at 90°, so it lands at angle 90° + θ: the point (cos(90° + θ), sin(90° + θ)) = −sin θcos θ. Those are the columns:

Q = cos θ−sin θsin θcos θ
For a quarter turn, θ = 90°: cos θ = 0 and sin θ = 1, so Q = 0−110. It sends 10 to 01 and 01 to −10.
Check with a picture: turn the arrow 10 a quarter turn counterclockwise and it points straight up, at 01. Turn 01 the same way and it points left, at −10. The formula agrees.

What happens to areas

Watch the shaded unit square in the lab. The matrix turns it into a parallelogram, and the area of that parallelogram is |det A| = |ps − qr|, with p, q, r, s named as on slide 3. Every other shape has its area multiplied by the same number (Strang §5.3).

The sign of det A says one more thing. A negative determinant means the plane has been flipped over, like graph paper turned face down. Slide 7 builds such a flip.

A rotation has det Q = cos²θ + sin²θ = 1. It turns the paper without stretching it.

Where this is used

A drone carries an IMU (inertial measurement unit), a small sensor chip that measures motion along the drone's own axes: its forward, sideways and up directions. A 3 by 3 rotation matrix turns those readings into north, east and up. The attitude estimator is the part of the flight software that tracks which way the drone is tilted and facing. Its job is to keep that rotation matrix up to date.
