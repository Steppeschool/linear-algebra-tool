Multiplying a matrix by a vector

A matrix A is a table of numbers, in rows and columns. To multiply it by a vector x = x₁x₂, there are two ways, and they always give the same answer. By rows: dot each row of A with x (slide 2), and each dot product gives one number of the answer. By columns: take x₁ of column 1, plus x₂ of column 2, a linear combination like on slide 1. Rows are the quick way to compute. Columns are the way to understand what Ax can be.

Lesson 1 · slide 3Matrix times vector
a₁₁a₁₂a₂₁a₂₂ x₁x₂ = x₁ a₁₁a₂₁ + x₂ a₁₂a₂₂
Key ideas
  1. Row way: entry i of Ax is row i of A dotted with x.
  2. Column way: Ax = x₁·(column 1) + x₂·(column 2), a combination of the columns.
  3. Both ways add up the same products, so they always agree.
  4. x needs one number per column of A. Ax has one number per row of A.

A note on writing: aij is the entry in row i, column j, so a₁₂ is in row 1, column 2. That is what the small numbers in the banner above mean.

One example, two ways

Call the matrix A, and the two numbers in x x₁ and x₂. Take

A = 2−111    x = 31

Way 1, by rows. Each row of A is dotted with x. Row 1 gives the top number of the answer, row 2 the bottom number:

row 1: 2−1 · 31 = 2·3 + (−1)·1 = 6 − 1 = 5
row 2: 11 · 31 = 1·3 + 1·1 = 3 + 1 = 4
Ax = 54

Way 2, by columns. x = 31 says: take 3 of column 1 and 1 of column 2, and add:

Ax = 3·21 + 1·−11 = 63 + −11 = 54

Same answer, 54. It has to be: both ways add the same four products, just in a different order. On the plane, the column way is a walk: 3 steps of 21, then 1 step of −11.

An everyday picture: a shopping basket

An egg has about 6 g of protein and 1 g of carbohydrate. A bowl of rice has about 4 g and 45 g. Each food is a column:

A = 64145    x = (2 eggs, 1 bowl)

The basket holds 2·61 + 1·445 = 1647: 16 g of protein, 47 g of carbohydrate. That total is Ax. Adding whole foods, one column at a time, is the column way.

The basket by rows

Or add one nutrient at a time. Protein: 6·2 + 4·1 = 16. Carbohydrate: 1·2 + 45·1 = 47. Each is a dot product of a row of A with x.

Both ways add the same products, so they always agree. Rows are quicker by hand. Columns show what Ax can be.

Watch the sizes

A vector with n entries is an n by 1 matrix. x needs one entry per column of A, and Ax has one entry per row. So a 2 by 3 matrix (2 rows, 3 columns) takes in 3 numbers and gives back 2.

(2 × 3) · (3 × 1) → (2 × 1)    the inner numbers must match, the outer numbers give the size of the answer

A bigger example: 4 nutrients, 3 foods

Add an apple, and track fat and fiber too. Now A has 4 rows (nutrients) and 3 columns (foods):

A = 64014525500014    x = (2 eggs, 1 bowl, 1 apple)
Ax = 2·6150 + 1·44501 + 1·02504 = 1672105

16 g protein, 72 g carbs, 10 g fat, 5 g fiber. Same idea, bigger table: 3 numbers go in, 4 come out, because (4 × 3) · (3 × 1) → (4 × 1). A matrix can have any number of rows and columns.

A10 = 1·(column 1) + 0·(column 2) = column 1
A01 = column 2

So the columns of A are exactly what A does to 10 and 01. We use this again in lesson 6, where a matrix moves the whole plane.
