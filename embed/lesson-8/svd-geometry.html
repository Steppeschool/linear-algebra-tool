Strang §7.4 · The geometry of the SVD

Every matrix: turn, stretch, turn.

Eigenvectors needed a square matrix and did not always exist. The singular value decomposition (SVD) works for every matrix. It says that any matrix is a turn, then a stretch along perpendicular axes, then another turn. The circle of unit vectors always becomes an ellipse.

Lesson 8 · slide 1Turn, stretch, turn
A = U Σ Vᵀ    U, V orthogonal (turns)    Σ diagonal, σ₁ ≥ σ₂ ≥ … ≥ 0 (stretches)
Key ideas
  1. Every matrix A can be written A = UΣVᵀ.
  2. Vᵀ turns (or mirrors) the input; Σ stretches along the axes; U turns (or mirrors) the result.
  3. The stretch factors σ₁ ≥ σ₂ ≥ … ≥ 0 are the singular values.
  4. A maps the unit circle to an ellipse whose half-axes have lengths σ₁ and σ₂. σ₁ is the most A ever stretches a unit vector.

Kneading dough

A baker turns a round piece of dough to a good angle, rolls it out in one direction and presses it in the other, then turns it again to fit the tray. Any 2 by 2 matrix does exactly this to the plane: turn, stretch along two perpendicular directions, turn. A round shape always comes out as an ellipse.

The circle becomes an ellipse

Take all unit vectors v, the unit circle. Multiply each by A. The results Av trace an ellipse. Its longest half-axis has length σ₁ and its shortest σ₂. These are the singular values. The unit vector v₁ that lands on the long axis is stretched the most: |Av₁| = σ₁.

A worked example

A = 2−211
v₁ = (1, −1)/√2:   Av₁ = (2 + 2, 1 − 1)/√2 = (4, 0)/√2 = (2√2, 0).   Stretched by σ₁ = 2√2 ≈ 2.83
v₂ = (1, 1)/√2:   Av₂ = (0, 2)/√2 = (0, √2).   Stretched by σ₂ = √2 ≈ 1.41
v₁ ⟂ v₂ and Av₁ ⟂ Av₂: two perpendicular directions go to two perpendicular directions

So this A turns the plane by 45° (taking (1, −1) to the x-axis), then stretches x by 2√2 and y by √2. Here U = I, so the last turn does nothing. Every other unit vector is stretched by something between √2 and 2√2.

The three factors

Write the input directions v₁, v₂ as the columns of V and the output directions u₁ = Av₁/σ₁, u₂ = Av₂/σ₂ as the columns of U. Both are orthogonal matrices (lesson 5): pure turns or mirrors (slide 2 shows why the u's always come out perpendicular). AV has columns Av₁ = σ₁u₁ and Av₂ = σ₂u₂, which is exactly UΣ. So AV = UΣ, and since V⁻¹ = Vᵀ:

A = UΣVᵀ,   Σ = σ₁00σ₂

Read it right to left, like any product: Vᵀ turns, Σ stretches, U turns.

Where this is used

For a robot arm, the matrix from joint speeds to hand speed maps a circle of joint speeds to an ellipse of hand speeds: the "manipulability ellipse". Its long axis is where the hand moves easily; a tiny σ₂ warns that the arm can hardly move in some direction.

Worked examples

Example 1 · A pure stretch

A = 3001.

The circle becomes an ellipse with half-axes 3 (along x) and 1 (along y)
σ₁ = 3, σ₂ = 1. U = V = I: no turning needed

Example 2 · A rotation

A rotation turns the circle into itself.

Every unit vector keeps length 1
σ₁ = σ₂ = 1. The ellipse is the circle

Example 3 · A matrix that flattens the plane

A = 1111.

v₁ = (1, 1)/√2: Av₁ = (2, 2)/√2, length 2.   v₂ = (1, −1)/√2: Av₂ = 0
σ₁ = 2, σ₂ = 0: the ellipse is squashed into a line segment

A zero singular value means A squashes a direction: rank 1.

What are the singular values of 0520?

A(1, 0) = (0, 2) and A(0, 1) = (5, 0): the two axes are stretched by 2 and 5 and stay perpendicular. σ₁ = 5, σ₂ = 2.

2

Why are singular values never negative, even though eigenvalues can be?

Show solutionHide solution

A singular value is a length, |Avᵢ|. Any flip is put into U or V (a mirror), so Σ only holds sizes.

3

A is 2 by 2 with σ₁ = 4 and σ₂ = 1. What is the area of the shape A makes from the unit square, and what is |det A|?

Show solutionHide solution

|det A| = σ₁σ₂ = 4: U and V are turns or mirrors (area 1), and Σ multiplies area by 4 · 1.

Worked examples

Example 1 · SVD of a tall matrix

A = 101101 (3 by 2).

AᵀA = 2112: λ = 3 with (1, 1)/√2,   λ = 1 with (1, −1)/√2
σ₁ = √3, σ₂ = 1
u₁ = Av₁/σ₁ = (1, 2, 1)/√6,   u₂ = Av₂/σ₂ = (1, 0, −1)/√2 (perpendicular ✓)

Example 2 · A rank-one matrix

A = 122436 = (1, 2, 3) times the row (1, 2).

One piece: σ₁ = |(1, 2, 3)| · |(1, 2)| = √14 · √5 = √70 ≈ 8.37,   σ₂ = 0
u₁ = (1, 2, 3)/√14,   v₁ = (1, 2)/√5

Example 3 · Pseudoinverse of a tall matrix

A = 100001, b = (1, 2, 3).

Both σ's are 1 and A⁺ = Aᵀ here: A⁺ = 100001,   x⁺ = (1, 3)
The middle equation 0 = 2 cannot be met; x⁺ satisfies the other two exactly

Problems

1

Find the singular values of 2112.

Show solutionHide solution

It is symmetric with eigenvalues 3 and 1, both positive, so σ₁ = 3 and σ₂ = 1.

2

Find the singular values of 403-5.

Show solutionHide solution

AᵀA = [25 −15; −15 25]: trace 50, det 625 − 225 = 400. λ² − 50λ + 400 = (λ − 40)(λ − 10). σ₁ = √40 ≈ 6.32, σ₂ = √10 ≈ 3.16. Check: σ₁σ₂ = 20 = |det A| ✓

3

Write 3311 as one piece σuvᵀ with unit vectors.

Show solutionHide solution

It is (3, 1) times the row (1, 1). σ = |(3, 1)| · |(1, 1)| = √10 · √2 = √20, u = (3, 1)/√10, v = (1, 1)/√2.

4

A 200 by 300 image has singular values that drop below 1% of σ₁ after the first 30. How many numbers does the rank-30 copy store?

Show solutionHide solution

30 · (200 + 300 + 1) = 15,030, compared with 60,000 for the full image: a quarter.

5

Centered data has singular values 6, 2, 1. What share of the spread do the first two principal components explain?

Show solutionHide solution

Squares 36, 4, 1, total 41. (36 + 4)/41 ≈ 97.6%.
