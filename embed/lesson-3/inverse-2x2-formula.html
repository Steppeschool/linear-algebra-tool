Strang §2.5 · The 2 by 2 inverse

Swap, flip signs, divide by the determinant.

A 2 by 2 matrix has a short inverse formula. Swap the two diagonal entries, flip the signs of the other two, and divide by the determinant ad − bc.

Lesson 3 · slide 22 × 2 inverse
abcd⁻¹ = 1/(ad − bc) · d−b−ca
Key ideas
  1. A⁻¹ = 1/(ad − bc) times [[d, −b], [−c, a]].
  2. A times [[d, −b], [−c, a]] is (ad − bc)I, so dividing by ad − bc leaves I.
  3. If ad − bc = 0 there is no inverse.
  4. Always check the answer by multiplying: AA⁻¹ should be I.

The recipe

abcd⁻¹ = 1/(ad − bc) · d−b−ca

Swap a and d. Flip the signs of b and c. Divide everything by ad − bc.

Why it works

Multiply A by the swapped-and-flipped matrix, before dividing:

abcdd−b−ca = ad − bc−ab + bacd − dc−cb + da = ad − bc00ad − bc
= (ad − bc) I

The off-diagonal entries cancel. Both diagonal entries are ad − bc. So the product is ad − bc times I. Divide by ad − bc and you get I.

When ad − bc = 0

The number ad − bc is called the determinant of A, written det A. It is the one number that decides whether a 2 by 2 matrix has an inverse. If ad − bc ≠ 0, the formula works. If ad − bc = 0, the formula would divide by zero, and there is no inverse.

What does det A = 0 look like? Then ad = bc, and the columns ac and bd lie on one line: one is a multiple of the other. That is lesson 1's dependent case. Two different inputs give the same output, so nothing can undo A. Lesson 6 shows what the determinant measures in pictures: how much A stretches areas.

A worked example

A = 2153   det A = 2·3 − 1·5 = 1
A⁻¹ = 1/1 · 3−1−52
Check: AA⁻¹ = 6 − 5−2 + 215 − 15−5 + 6 = 1001 ✓

Common mistakes

  • Swapping b and c. Only the diagonal entries, a and d, trade places.
  • Flipping the signs of a and d. Only b and c change sign.
  • Forgetting to divide by det A. The example hides this, since det A = 1.

Bigger matrices

Bigger matrices have a formula too (lesson 6, Strang chapter 5), but it is far too slow to use. In practice the inverse comes from elimination (slide 4).
