Key ideas
  1. A⁻¹ undoes A, and exists exactly when det A ≠ 0.
  2. For 2 by 2: swap, flip signs, divide by ad − bc. In general: Gauss-Jordan.
  3. (AB)⁻¹ = B⁻¹A⁻¹ and (AB)ᵀ = BᵀAᵀ: the order reverses.
  4. Permutations reorder rows, P⁻¹ = Pᵀ, and PA = LU.
  • §2.5 The inverse. A⁻¹ plays A backwards. It exists exactly when A has n pivots, or det A ≠ 0.
  • §2.5 The 2 by 2 formula. Swap the diagonal, flip the other signs, divide by ad − bc. That number is the determinant: ad − bc = 0 means the columns lie on one line and there is no inverse.
  • §2.5 Products. Shoes off before socks: (AB)⁻¹ = B⁻¹A⁻¹.
  • §2.5 Gauss-Jordan. Carbon paper: eliminate on [A | I] until it reads [I | A⁻¹].
  • §2.7 Transposes. Turn the spreadsheet. Distance tables are symmetric, and AᵀA always is.
  • §2.7 Permutations. A new playlist order. P⁻¹ = Pᵀ, and row exchanges give PA = LU.
