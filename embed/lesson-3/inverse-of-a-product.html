Strang §2.5 · Inverse of a product

Inverse of a product: undo the steps in reverse order

Slides 1 and 2 undid one matrix. Now two matrices act one after the other. In the morning you put on socks, then shoes. In the evening you take off the shoes first, then the socks: the same steps, in the opposite order. Matrices work the same way. The product AB means "do B first, then A", because B sits next to the vector. To undo it, first undo A, the last step, and then undo B. So the inverse of a product is (AB)⁻¹ = B⁻¹A⁻¹, with the order reversed. Get the order wrong and you do not get back to the start.

Lesson 3 · slide 3Inverse of AB
(AB)⁻¹ = B⁻¹A⁻¹     (ABC)⁻¹ = C⁻¹B⁻¹A⁻¹
Key ideas
  1. ABx means: B acts on x first, then A acts on the result.
  2. (AB)⁻¹ = B⁻¹A⁻¹. The order reverses: the last step done is the first undone.
  3. For three matrices: (ABC)⁻¹ = C⁻¹B⁻¹A⁻¹.
  4. The check: B⁻¹A⁻¹AB = B⁻¹IB = B⁻¹B = I.

Socks and shoes with row steps

Take two row steps, like those in lesson 2. Each one is a matrix acting on the left, like lesson 2's elimination matrices. Step E: row 2 − 3·row 1. Step F: row 1 + 2·row 2. Do E first, then F, on the matrix 1235. Then undo them: first undo F (row 1 − 2·row 2), then undo E (row 2 + 3·row 1).

1235  → E →  120−1  → F →  100−1
100−1  → undo F →  120−1  → undo E →  1235 ✓ back to the start

Now undo them in the wrong order, undo-E first:

100−1  → undo E →  103−1  → undo F →  −523−1 ✗

The same rule with matrices

Matrix products act right to left: the step nearest the matrix acts first, so E then F is written FE. The right undo was E⁻¹F⁻¹. In general, in ABx, B is next to x, so B acts first. A went on last, so A⁻¹ must act first. In B⁻¹A⁻¹ the factor on the right, A⁻¹, acts first, which is what we need. The algebra check:

(B⁻¹A⁻¹)(AB) = B⁻¹(A⁻¹A)B = B⁻¹ I B = B⁻¹B = I ✓

A meets A⁻¹ in the middle, and they cancel. Then B and B⁻¹ cancel. In the other order, A⁻¹B⁻¹AB, nothing meets its own inverse, and the result is usually not I.

A worked example: two simple steps

B = 1101 adds the second entry to the first (slide 1). A = 2001 doubles the first entry. Do B first, then A:

AB = 20011101 = 2201
B⁻¹A⁻¹ = 1−101½001 = ½−101
(B⁻¹A⁻¹)(AB) = ½·2 + (−1)·0½·2 + (−1)·101 = 1001 ✓

The wrong order, A⁻¹B⁻¹, fails:

A⁻¹B⁻¹ = ½0011−101 = ½−½01
(A⁻¹B⁻¹)(AB) = ½·2½·2 − ½·101 = 1½01 ≠ I ✗

Another example: R and S from slide 1

R = 0−110 sends pq to −qp (slide 1's examples). S = 1101 adds the second entry to the first (slide 1). Multiplying as in lesson 2, RS = 0−111.
R⁻¹ = 01−10   S⁻¹ = 1−101 (both from slide 1)
S⁻¹R⁻¹ = 11−10   but   R⁻¹S⁻¹ = 01−11
Check: (S⁻¹R⁻¹)(RS) = 1·0 + 1·11·(−1) + 1·1−1·0 + 0·1(−1)(−1) + 0·1 = 1001 ✓
Only S⁻¹R⁻¹ undoes RS. In the lab, follow v = 11 through S and R, then try both orders on the way back.

A bigger example: three 3 × 3 steps

Lesson 2 eliminated with three steps, E₃₂E₃₁E₂₁A = U. E₂₁ subtracts 2·row 1 from row 2, E₃₁ adds row 1 to row 3, and E₃₂ adds row 2 to row 3. To undo all three, reverse the order and undo each one:

(E₃₂E₃₁E₂₁)⁻¹ = E₂₁⁻¹ E₃₁⁻¹ E₃₂⁻¹
= 100210001 100010−101 1000100−11 = 100210−1−11 = L

That is lesson 2's L, the matrix in A = LU. Each undo flips the sign of its multiplier, and in this reversed order the multipliers drop into L without mixing (see lesson 2, slide 7). A computer chaining a thousand steps undoes them the same way: last step first.

Where this is used

A robot arm chains joint moves, one after another. To reverse them, undo the last joint first.
