Key ideas
  1. Eigenvectors keep their direction; λ is the stretch.
  2. Eigenvalues solve det(A − λI) = 0; they add to the trace and multiply to det.
  3. A = XΛX⁻¹ makes powers easy; |λ| decides growth or decay.
  4. Symmetric matrices have perpendicular eigenvectors; positive definite means all λ > 0.
  • §6.1 Eigenvectors. The rubber sheet: directions that only stretch. Ax = λx.
  • §6.1 Finding them. Turn the λ dial until A − λI is singular: det(A − λI) = 0, then a null space for each λ.
  • §6.2 Diagonalizing. Turning the map: A = XΛX⁻¹ and Aᵏ = XΛᵏX⁻¹.
  • §6.2 Powers and stability. Two cities: parts with |λ| < 1 die out, λ = 1 is the steady state.
  • §6.4 Symmetric matrices. A sheet pulled two ways: real λ, perpendicular eigenvectors, A = QΛQᵀ.
  • §6.5 Positive definite. A bowl, not a saddle: all λ > 0, xᵀAx > 0.
