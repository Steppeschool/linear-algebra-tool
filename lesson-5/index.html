Orthogonality and least squares

Perpendicular subspaces, projections, the best answer when Ax = b has no solution, fitting lines to data, and orthonormal bases built by Gram-Schmidt. 8 short slides, then worked examples and problems. Each slide has an everyday picture, a worked example and Python you can run right on the page.

Slides

01 · Strang §4.1 · Orthogonality of the four subspaces

Orthogonal Vectors and Subspaces Explained Visually

Perpendicular vectors have dot product zero. The row space is perpendicular to the null space, and the column space to the left null space. Examples and Python. Strang §4.1.

02 · Strang §4.2 · Projections

Projection Onto a Line: Formula and Picture

The projection of b onto the line through a is the closest point p = (aᵀb / aᵀa) a. The error b − p is perpendicular to a. Worked examples and Python. Strang §4.2.

03 · Strang §4.2 · Projection matrices

Projection Matrix P = A(AᵀA)⁻¹Aᵀ Explained

The projection onto a line is Pb with P = aaᵀ/aᵀa. Onto the column space of A, P = A(AᵀA)⁻¹Aᵀ. Why P² = P, with examples and Python. Strang §4.2.

04 · Strang §4.3 · Least squares approximations

Least Squares and the Normal Equations AᵀAx = Aᵀb

When Ax = b has no solution, least squares finds the x̂ that makes the error smallest by solving AᵀAx̂ = Aᵀb. Why it works, worked examples and Python. Strang §4.3.

05 · Strang §4.3 · Fitting a straight line

Fitting a Line to Data with Least Squares (Interactive)

Fit the best straight line C + Dt to noisy sensor data. Drag C and D to shrink the squared error, then solve the normal equations. Sensor calibration example. Strang §4.3.

06 · Strang §4.4 · Orthonormal bases

Orthonormal Basis and Orthogonal Matrices (QᵀQ = I)

Orthonormal vectors are perpendicular and of length 1. In the columns of Q they give QᵀQ = I, Q⁻¹ = Qᵀ and length-preserving rotations. Examples and Python. Strang §4.4.

07 · Strang §4.4 · The Gram-Schmidt process

Gram-Schmidt Process Step by Step (and A = QR)

Gram-Schmidt turns independent vectors into an orthonormal basis: subtract projections, then divide by length. Recording the steps gives A = QR. Examples and Python. Strang §4.4.
