Strang §7.1 · Image processing by linear algebra

Compressing an image: keep the big pieces.

A grey picture is a matrix of brightness values. Its SVD lists the picture's pieces from most to least important. Keep the first few pieces and you keep most of the picture while storing far fewer numbers. Pictures with simple structure need very few pieces.

Lesson 8 · slide 4Image compression
A ≈ A_k = σ₁u₁v₁ᵀ + … + σₖuₖvₖᵀ    error set by the dropped σ's
Key ideas
  1. A grey image with m rows and n columns of pixels is an m by n matrix.
  2. A_k, the first k SVD pieces, is the closest matrix of rank k to A (the Eckart–Young theorem).
  3. Storing A_k needs k(m + n + 1) numbers instead of mn.
  4. Fast-falling singular values mean the image compresses well.

A sketch artist

A good sketch artist draws the big shapes first, then the main lines, and only then the fine detail. After a few strokes you already recognise the face. The SVD orders an image the same way: the first piece is the biggest pattern, each later piece adds smaller detail.

Pictures are matrices

Give each pixel a number from 0 (white) to 1 (black). An 8 by 8 picture of the letter F becomes an 8 by 8 matrix of 0s and 1s. A photo of 1000 by 1000 pixels is a 1000 by 1000 matrix: a million numbers.

Keep k pieces

A_k = σ₁u₁v₁ᵀ + σ₂u₂v₂ᵀ + … + σₖuₖvₖᵀ
Each piece costs m + n + 1 numbers (u, v and σ)
1000 by 1000 photo, k = 50: 50 · 2001 ≈ 100,000 numbers instead of 1,000,000

No other matrix of rank k is closer to A than A_k. The error comes only from the dropped pieces: the most the copy can be off in any direction is σₖ₊₁, the largest dropped σ. When the dropped σ's are small, the copy is good.
