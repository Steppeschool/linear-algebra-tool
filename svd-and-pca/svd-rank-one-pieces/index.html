Strang §7.2 · The pieces of the SVD

Splitting a matrix into rank-one pieces

Slide 2 found U, Σ and V. Now we multiply them back together in a clever order. Think of a song made from separate tracks: drums, bass, voice. Each track is simple on its own, and the song is all of them played at once. If you mute the quiet tracks, you still know the song. The SVD splits any matrix the same way. Each piece σuvᵀ is a very simple matrix, a column times a row, called a rank-one matrix. Add the pieces and you get A back. And they come loudest first, because σ₁ ≥ σ₂ ≥ ….

Lesson 8 · slide 3Rank-one pieces
A = σ₁u₁v₁ᵀ + σ₂u₂v₂ᵀ + … + σᵣuᵣvᵣᵀ
Key ideas
  1. UΣVᵀ = σ₁u₁v₁ᵀ + … + σᵣuᵣvᵣᵀ, where r is the rank (the number of nonzero σ's).
  2. Each uvᵀ is a rank-one matrix: a column times a row. All its rows are multiples of vᵀ.
  3. The pieces are ordered: σ₁ ≥ σ₂ ≥ …, so the first pieces carry the most.
  4. Keeping only the first k pieces gives the best rank-k approximation of A: the closest matrix of rank k (slide 4).

Column times row

Lesson 2 multiplied matrices as row times column. Turn it around: a column u times a row vᵀ. Each entry is one number of u times one number of v. The answer is a whole matrix uvᵀ, called an outer product.

u = 12, v = 31:   uvᵀ = 12 31 = 1·31·12·32·1 = 3162
Row 2 = 2 × row 1.   Column 1 = 3 × column 2.   Rank 1

Every row is a multiple of vᵀ, and every column is a multiple of u. So all the outputs lie on one line, the line through u. That is what rank 1 means.

The SVD as a sum

Multiply U times ΣVᵀ the column-times-row way: column i of U times row i of ΣVᵀ, added up. Row i of ΣVᵀ is σᵢvᵢᵀ, so each term is σᵢuᵢvᵢᵀ:

A = UΣVᵀ = σ₁u₁v₁ᵀ + σ₂u₂v₂ᵀ + … + σᵣuᵣvᵣᵀ

Pieces with σ = 0 add nothing, so there are exactly r pieces, where r is the rank. A quick check with slide 1's matrix, where u₁ = 10, u₂ = 01:

σ₁u₁v₁ᵀ = 2√2 · 10 1/√2−1/√2 = 2−200
σ₂u₂v₂ᵀ = √2 · 01 1/√21/√2 = 0011
Sum: 2−211 = A ✓

A worked example

A = 1221: eigenvalues 3 with 11/√2 and −1 with 1−1/√2
σ₁ = 3:   v₁ = u₁ = 11/√2,   3u₁v₁ᵀ = 3 · ½1111 = 1.51.51.51.5
σ₂ = 1:   v₂ = 1−1/√2, u₂ = Av₂ = −11/√2 (the eigenvalue −1 puts its minus sign into u₂),   1·u₂v₂ᵀ = −0.50.50.5−0.5
Sum: 1221 ✓

The first piece alone, all 1.5's, is the best rank-one guess at A. It keeps the long axis of the ellipse and squashes the short one to nothing:

The second piece puts back the missing short axis. How far off is the guess? The error is exactly the dropped piece, and its size is σ₂ = 1.
