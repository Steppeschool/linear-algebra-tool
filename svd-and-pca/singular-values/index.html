Strang §7.2 · Bases and matrices in the SVD

Finding σ: the eigenvalues of AᵀA, square-rooted.

AᵀA is symmetric, so it has perpendicular eigenvectors and real eigenvalues. Those eigenvalues are never negative: if AᵀAv = λv with |v| = 1, then λ = vᵀAᵀAv = |Av|² ≥ 0. Its eigenvectors are the input directions v, and the square roots of its eigenvalues are the singular values. Then each output direction is u = Av / σ.

Lesson 8 · slide 2Singular values
AᵀA vᵢ = σᵢ² vᵢ    σᵢ = √λᵢ(AᵀA)    uᵢ = Avᵢ / σᵢ
Key ideas
  1. AᵀA = VΣᵀΣVᵀ: its eigenvectors are the vᵢ and its eigenvalues are σᵢ².
  2. So σᵢ = √(eigenvalue of AᵀA). These eigenvalues are never negative.
  3. The output directions are uᵢ = Avᵢ/σᵢ, automatically perpendicular and of length 1.
  4. Similarly AAᵀ has eigenvectors uᵢ and the same nonzero eigenvalues σᵢ².

Measure the stretch, then take the root

To find how much A stretches a unit vector v, look at the squared length: |Av|² = (Av)ᵀ(Av) = vᵀ(AᵀA)v. So everything about stretching lives in the matrix AᵀA. It is symmetric (lesson 7), so it has perpendicular eigenvectors. Its eigenvalues are squared lengths |Av|², so they are never negative.

Why the eigenvectors of AᵀA are the right v's

A = UΣVᵀ,   so Aᵀ = VΣᵀUᵀ
AᵀA = VΣᵀUᵀUΣVᵀ = V(ΣᵀΣ)Vᵀ,   because UᵀU = I
This is QΛQᵀ from lesson 7 with Q = V and Λ = ΣᵀΣ = diag(σ₁², σ₂², …). (Σ is rectangular when A is, so we write ΣᵀΣ, a square diagonal matrix.)

So: eigenvectors of AᵀA give V, and the square roots of its eigenvalues give the σ's. Then uᵢ = Avᵢ/σᵢ for every nonzero σᵢ. (When σᵢ = 0 there is nothing to divide; any unit vectors that complete U to an orthogonal matrix will do.) Also σ₁σ₂ = |det A| for a 2 by 2 matrix, because the turns U and V do not change area.

A worked example

A = 2−211 (slide 1),   AᵀA = 5−3−35
trace 10, det 25 − 9 = 16:   λ² − 10λ + 16 = (λ − 8)(λ − 2),   λ = 8 and 2
σ₁ = √8 = 2√2 with v₁ = (1, −1)/√2,   σ₂ = √2 with v₂ = (1, 1)/√2 ✓ (as found by turning v on slide 1)
u₁ = Av₁/σ₁ = (2√2, 0)/(2√2) = (1, 0),   u₂ = (0, √2)/√2 = (0, 1)
